Under Mohammed Karim Chowdhury's leadership, ASAAL is investing in the next generation — expanding the Youth Committee scholarship program, deepening chapter infrastructure across the South and Midwest, and strengthening the legislative endorsement process that has already produced a generation of South Asian American officials at the City, State, and Federal levels.

§ Biography

Building on the founding vision.

Mohammed Karim Chowdhury serves as the National President of the Alliance of South Asian American Labor (ASAAL). He was elected by the membership to lead the federation's day-to-day national leadership, working alongside Founder & President Emeritus Maf Misbah Uddin and an Executive Council of eleven officers. He is also a member of the Board of Trustees — a continuity of service that connects the founding generation to the leadership of today.

His path to the presidency runs through electoral organizing at the borough, city, and state levels. He is a member of the Richmond County Democratic Committee — bringing party-level relationships and electoral discipline directly into ASAAL's endorsement process. He served on the Mayoral Transition Team, placing South Asian American voices inside the formation of a New York City administration. That blend of party and movement work shapes how he leads ASAAL today.

Under his leadership, ASAAL is investing in three priorities. First, the Youth Committee — expanding the scholarship program that recognizes the next generation of South Asian American leaders, and building a real pipeline from high-school membership into civic and electoral roles. Second, chapter growth — opening new chapters in the South and Midwest, where the South Asian American population is growing fastest, while strengthening the infrastructure of existing chapters across all 11 states. Third, the legislative endorsement process — preparing for the 2026 cycle with a strengthened vetting, endorsement, and mobilization framework that builds on ASAAL's record of historic firsts.

ASAAL's electoral history forms the floor under that work. The federation helped elect Grace Meng as the first Asian Congresswoman from the East (2012); the first Bangladeshi-American State Senator from Georgia (2018); and three South Asian officials in New York City on a single ballot in 2021, including the first Bangladeshi-American Civil Court Judge. The next chapter of that record is being written now.
