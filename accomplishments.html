2008 — Founding

ASAAL is established.

The Alliance of South Asian American Labor is founded as the first and only national organization of the South Asian diaspora living in America. Eight founding communities — Afghan, Bangladeshi, Bhutanese, Indian, Maldivian, Nepalese, Pakistani, and Sri Lankan — finally have an independent voice in American labor and civic life.

2012 — Federal first

Helping elect the first Asian Congresswoman from the East.

ASAAL doorknocking, phone banking, and voter mobilization helps elect Grace Meng as the first Asian Congresswoman representing an East-Coast district — a milestone for AAPI political representation in Washington.

June 13, 2017 — AFL-CIO affiliation

Constituency group of the NY State AFL-CIO.

ASAAL officially becomes a constituency group of the New York State AFL-CIO — a federation of 3,000 affiliated public sector, private sector, and building trades unions throughout the state, representing 2.5 million members. This is the largest State Federation of the AFL-CIO in the United States.

May 22, 2018 — Statewide

First Bangladeshi-American State Senator from Georgia.

ASAAL helps elect a South Asian of Bangladeshi origin as a State Senator from the Red State of Georgia. Proof that South Asian American power isn't confined to the coasts.

November 2, 2021 — NYC

Three South Asians elected in New York City.

In a historic election day, ASAAL helps elect three South Asian Americans across NYC — including the first Bangladeshi-American Civil Court Judge and a new City Council Member. Years of organizing pay off in a single ballot.

2022 – 2024 — National expansion

From 18 chapters to 20 chapters across 11 states.

ASAAL crosses 18 chapters in ten states — nine in New York and one each in New Jersey, Georgia, Michigan, Pennsylvania, Florida, Virginia, Maryland, California, and Washington DC — then adds Houston (TX) and the Healthcare Professionals chapter. Cross-coalition work with CBTU, LCLAA, APALA, and the NAACP deepens.

2025 – 2026 — Today

A movement of our own. Ten thousand members strong.

ASAAL crosses 10,000 members, hosts endorsement events with elected officials including Assemblymember Zohran Mamdani, and prepares for the 18th National Convention. The work continues — voter registration, advocacy, coalition building, and electing leaders who fight for South Asian working families.
