§ Leadership journey

From a single chapter to a national federation.

2008 · Founding

Founded the Alliance of South Asian American Labor.

Convened the founding members in New York with a vision that has held steady ever since: the first and only national organization of the South Asian diaspora in America, rooted in labor, organized for political and social justice.

2008 – 2016 · Build

Chapter-by-chapter, state-by-state.

Built ASAAL's first chapters in Queens, Brooklyn, Bronx, Manhattan, Jackson Heights, Richmond Hill, Ozone Park, Long Island, and the NY Capital region — then expanded into New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Georgia, Michigan, Florida, Virginia, Maryland, DC, and California.

2012 · Federal

Helped elect the first Asian Congresswoman from the East.

ASAAL members and chapters mobilized for Grace Meng's historic election — a turning point that proved the South Asian American vote could decide federal elections in the Eastern United States.

2017 · Affiliation

Led ASAAL into the NY State AFL-CIO.

On June 13, 2017, ASAAL officially became a constituency group of the New York State AFL-CIO — a federation of 3,000 affiliated unions representing 2.5 million members. The largest validation yet of South Asian American labor as a movement.

2018 · Statewide

First Bangladeshi-American State Senator in Georgia.

Coordinated a multi-state mobilization that elected the first South Asian of Bangladeshi origin to the Georgia State Senate — proving ASAAL's reach extended well beyond the Northeast.

2021 · NYC sweep

Three South Asians elected on a single ballot.

Including the first Bangladeshi-American Civil Court Judge and a new City Council Member — a single-cycle result that reflected more than a decade of organizing groundwork.

2022 · Recognition

Appointed to the NYS AAPI Commission.

Brought ASAAL's voice directly into the New York State Asian American & Pacific Islander Commission — extending the federation's policy reach into the executive branch.

Today

President Emeritus & Trustee.

Continues to serve on the Board of Trustees, mentor the current Executive Council under National President Mohammed Karim Chowdhury, and represent ASAAL nationally — anchoring continuity between the founding generation and the leadership of today.

"Afghans, Bangladeshis, Bhutanese, Indians, Maldivians, Nepalese, Pakistanis, and Sri Lankans now have a platform to express their voices, seek demands, and volunteer to bring real results for our community." — ASAAL Founding Statement, 2008
§ Biography

A life in labor and community.

Maf Misbah Uddin is the Founder of the Alliance of South Asian American Labor (ASAAL) and serves today as National President Emeritus and Trustee. He convened ASAAL's founding members in 2008 with a clear conviction: that the South Asian American community — the fastest-growing population in the United States — required an independent labor-based national voice. From a single chapter in Queens, the federation he built has grown to 20 chapters across 11 states with more than 10,000 members.

His leadership at ASAAL is grounded in a career in organized labor. He serves as President of Local 1407 and Treasurer of DC 37 — the largest municipal employees union in New York City, representing more than 150,000 members. That experience shaped the principle he carried into ASAAL's founding: that working people, organized together, are the engine of every just movement.

Under his leadership, ASAAL became a constituency group of the New York State AFL-CIO on June 13, 2017 — joining the 2.5-million-member state federation as the recognized national voice of the South Asian American workforce. He has been appointed to the New York State Asian American & Pacific Islander Commission, extending ASAAL's voice into state-level policymaking.

Politically, ASAAL has been at the center of cycle after cycle of historic firsts: helping elect Grace Meng as the first Asian Congresswoman from the East (2012), the first Bangladeshi-American State Senator from Georgia (2018), three South Asian officials in New York City on a single ballot in 2021, and a deep bench of judges, council members, and assembly representatives in the years between.

As President Emeritus, he continues to anchor the federation between Conventions — mentoring the Executive Council under National President Mohammed Karim Chowdhury, serving on the Board of Trustees, and representing ASAAL in coalition with CBTU, LCLAA, APALA, and the NAACP. The independent voice he set out to build in 2008 now speaks for a community of more than 10,000 organized members — and counts every year as the next chapter of the work.
